# Alchemy materials: candidate readings and limits

The new result is a stricter distinction between a **name**, a **candidate material**, and an **assayed composition**. The [machine dictionary](alchemy-dictionary.json) supplies modern notation while retaining ambiguity. Nothing here establishes the composition of a historical sample.

[Newton’s laboratory notebook](https://newton.dlib.indiana.edu/text/ALCH00110/normalized), selected folios 65r–67r, records different residues and appearances and contains tentative, revised interpretations. These passages strengthen the prior v3 warning against a universal decoder. Only the selected scholarly transcription was read; autograph images and the entire notebook were not collated. Historical taste observations are evidence about the text, not a modern analytical method.

The following compact mappings use the [Indiana University editorial glossary](https://newton.dlib.indiana.edu/page/glossary) and the notebook context. A formula translates a candidate chemical name into modern notation. It does not prove sample purity, oxidation state, crystal form or elemental/isotopic change.

## How to make a defensible identification

1. Record manuscript, folio, language, surrounding operation and alternatives before assigning a literal substance.
2. Separate elemental content from compound and phase. A detected element does not determine its compound; colour does not determine either.
3. Use characterized reference data and independent methods. An elemental signal and a phase-sensitive signal answer different questions; duplicates of the same channel do not remove an inverse-map ambiguity.
4. Leave an unresolved candidate unresolved when the record lacks distinguishing observations. No transmutation follows from colour, mass loss, crystallization or a star-shaped surface alone.

In this program, now focused on electricity and magnetism, alchemy contributes **measurement discipline and hypothesis generation**. The lowest-material work is digital text collation and reference-data inference. No hazardous historical preparation is proposed. Modern acoustic recordings cannot identify the elements in an unmeasured alchemical sample.

The dictionary is a source-bound curation contribution to this project, not a new chemical theory. The candidates and ambiguity rules are useful for annotating diagrams and preventing a rendered atom model from masquerading as an assay. A diagram should label the chosen candidate and uncertainty directly.
