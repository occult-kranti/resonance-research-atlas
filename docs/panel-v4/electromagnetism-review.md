## Current primary work and what it permits

[Chen et al., 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11071-024-10018-x), selected abstract/introduction and §2.1, offers a concrete graphite levitation model. Magnetic support, nonlinear stiffness and dissipation matter. Their precision instrument requirements differ from a small qualitative magnet-and-graphite demonstration. The useful next step here is a simulation with declared geometry and susceptibility, followed by an optical displacement check if physical equipment becomes available.

[Tajmar, Kößling and Neunzig, 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.15640), author PDF selected §§2–4, supplies the strongest direct metrology constraint. Their investigated steady-field arrangements did not yield an identified anomalous force. Stray magnetic interaction and outgassing remain relevant to the reported bounds. The study does not establish this project's sensitivity, and its static-field scope does not test every possible dynamic hypothesis. Publisher/PMC access was unsuccessful; the author PDF was read.

[Hermansen, Durhuus and Bjørk, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04410v1), selected setup and Eqs.1–7, shows a different class of magnetic levitation: driven rotation, inertia and a static on-axis component. Gravity remains in the equations. A stability simulation is a useful later benchmark; an exposed high-speed magnet assembly is not the selected minimal-material build.

[Zhang et al., 2026 v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19171v2) is an **abstract-only** current lead concerning cavity-associated superconductivity. Its transition temperature, field profile and detuning are separate observables from gravity or delivered work. The abstract's percentage and stated endpoints disagree; preserve this discrepancy rather than repairing it silently. Full methods and data have not been reviewed. The separate Nature DOI surfaced by the same lead could not be retrieved and is not treated as read evidence.

## Prospective minimum-material protocol

The following is a proposed low-voltage or simulated protocol, not a claim to reproduce the high-voltage/vacuum papers. The numerical contract fixes units, parameter ranges, model limits and acceptance thresholds before results are judged.

1. **Define the weighed and powered boundary.** Draw the coil, sample, supports, leads, battery/supply, sensor and any external magnet. State which reactions reach the scale and which reach another support.
2. **Close the electrical budget first.** Log synchronized voltage and current; retain endpoint capacitor and inductor energy. Integrate signed power rather than multiplying separate RMS values. Add known channel skew as a failure case.
3. **Add motion deliberately.** For a linear inductor with specified position-dependent inductance, include its motional voltage and the mechanical work. Separately challenge current reversal, position reversal, zero gradient and fixed position. Hysteresis and saturation require additional loss models.
4. **Make known ordinary forces observable.** Vary lead routing, external-field orientation and source/sample separation. Keep the source reaction visible through a separate support measurement or a complete-system force measurement. An even-in-current force can arise from ordinary induced magnetism, so polarity parity alone is insufficient.
5. **Separate heating and position drift.** Use a matched electrical-heating dummy, retain on/off and cooling segments, track optical position and temperature, and randomize or blind condition order. Record all runs and predeclare outlier criteria. Avoid choosing an especially quiet interval after looking at the result.
6. **Calibrate around the experiment.** Check force/displacement response before and after the sequence; use an independently supported reference with measured electromagnetic sensitivity. A nominally nonmagnetic material is not automatically a gravity-only witness.
7. **State the result at measured resolution.** Report estimated contrast, uncertainty, nuisance bounds and the tested region. A simulation validates calculation against its specified equations. A future unresolved physical residual is a reason to revise the model and controls; it is not by itself gravity modification.

Steps 1–7 are this panel's proposed synthesis. They are not quoted as a single source's protocol. Source-specific selected methods are recorded individually in the JSON dataset.
