# N4 source record

**NANO-FARKAS2025:** Natalia Farkas, John A. Kramar, Antonio R. Montoro Bustos,
George Caceres, Monique Johnson, Matthias Roesslein and Elijah J. Petersen,
“Derivation of Particle Number Concentration from the Size Distribution: Theory
and Applications,” *Analytical Chemistry*97(21)(2025),10999–11006.
https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.analchem.4c05990 . NIST primary landing page:
https://www.nist.gov/publications/derivation-particle-number-concentration-size-distribution-theory-and-applications .
Author PDF https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=958200 .
The historical/source researcher and advisor inspected the relevant moment/count
equations and correction equation, including PDF pages4 and7. This benchmark
reproduces the known need for an appropriate particle-volume moment; no experimental
dataset from the paper is reproduced and the correction is not claimed invented.

**ROS-2002:** Rosensweig, *JMMM*252(2002),370–374,
https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-8853(02)00706-0 . The conditional dilute small-field
Langevin susceptibility gives chi_i proportional to number_i*mu_i²; with equal
core magnetization density mu_i proportional to Vcore_i, hence chi_i proportional
to number_i*Vcore_i². The Farkas paper is not cited as establishing these magnetic
weights. They are a separate explicitly conditional equilibrium-response model.

Particle-number fractions, mass fractions, susceptibility fractions and
intensity-weighted optical size estimates must not be conflated. The synthetic
core and hydrodynamic diameters do not identify a material or establish blocked
moments, weak-field behavior, sphericity or interaction-free response in reality.
