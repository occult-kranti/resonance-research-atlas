# N3 sources and model provenance

**ROS-2002:** Rosensweig, *JMMM*252(2002),370–374,
https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-8853(02)00706-0 . Primary §§2–4 independently read
by the source researcher and advisor. Adopt the spherical Brownian time
tauB=3 eta Vhyd/(kB T) and approximate parallel-channel inverse-rate law in its
declared weak-field setting. We do not infer a Néel material parameter from size.

**NANO-GOTO2025:** Goto et al., “Effects of Néel and Brownian relaxations on dynamic
magnetization empirically characterized in single-core and multicore structures
of magnetic nanoparticles,” *Nanoscale*17(2025),12817–12825.
https://doi.org/10.1039/D5NR00722D ; primary publisher page:
https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2025/nr/d5nr00722d .
The source researcher inspected the accepted manuscript's introduction Eqs1–3,
Methods2.1–2.4, Results3.3–3.5 and conclusion/data statements. Its liquid-viscosity
and solid controls motivate system-specific mechanism comparisons; no raw data
were downloaded or reproduced here.

Section3.3's prose about peak frequency being proportional to viscosity was
reported by the reader as conflicting with tauB proportional to eta. The figure's
direction was not independently checked, so that phrase is not adopted as data or
treated as a verified source error. N3 derives its direction algebraically from
the stated model. The single- and two-population candidates are project test
fixtures, not fits to the paper's actual materials.

The single-frequency rival is allowed an unknown fitted susceptibility amplitude.
If an experiment independently calibrates that amplitude, one complex observation
can already impose an additional constraint. The simulator knowing its synthetic
true chi0 does not grant that knowledge to the fitted rival.
