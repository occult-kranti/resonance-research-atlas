# Dream and body-location research preparation

Prepared for the requested final sixth round. Status: source reading and proposed protocol only; no sixth-round production run or participant experiment has occurred.

## Distinct targets

| Target | What would count as data | What it cannot establish alone |
|---|---|---|
| Dream recall | Timestamped report, including no-recall nights | Accurate reconstruction of every sleep experience |
| Self-reported lucidity | Report that the participant knew they were dreaming | Polysomnographically verified REM or an external event |
| Laboratory lucidity | Prespecified physiological response plus independent sleep-state assessment | A universal brain frequency or hidden energy |
| Out-of-body phenomenology | Reported self-location, perspective and vividness | Movement of a body/soul or access to hidden information |
| External-target information | Locked, prespecified guess compared with concealed randomized target | A specific mechanism from a positive statistical result alone |

Konkoly et al. demonstrate a concrete, ordinary sensory communication channel with some lucid dreamers under laboratory monitoring. That should not be relabeled remote communication without sensory input. Ehrsson's experiment demonstrates that controlled multisensory cues can alter experienced body location. It tests an experience, not geographical separation of consciousness.

## Sleep-friendly observational session

This is a proposed observation workflow, not a tested induction or treatment.

1. Before the usual bedtime, choose a fixed observation period and create a dated entry. Record ordinary sleep context and a neutral intention to remember any dreams. Keep the usual sleep schedule.
2. On natural morning waking, record remembered content before reading targets or suggested meanings. Preserve an empty/no-recall entry when appropriate.
3. Separately mark recall, whether awareness of dreaming occurred, experienced perspective/self-location, confidence, and approximate sleep/wake times. Leave uncertain states uncertain; do not infer REM from a timer or an audio track.
4. Lock the first report. Keep later interpretation as a second field with its own timestamp so revisions do not rewrite what was originally recalled.
5. Review all scheduled entries, including misses, uneventful nights and missing data. A journal measures reports, not clinical outcomes or astral travel.

No stimulation, sleep deprivation, induced paralysis, flashing cues, electrical exposure, supplement regimen, or forced awakening is needed for this observational design.

## Proposed external-target test

Choose four equally likely target categories and a fixed number of scheduled trials before data collection. A separate custodian generates and conceals the target for each trial, with a locked record of the draw. The participant and scorer must not obtain target information before the response is locked. Log all scheduled trials and exclusions prospectively. Use exact category matching as the primary score; a later poetic or semantic resemblance is exploratory and needs a separate correction.

Under independent uniform target draws with one locked choice per trial and no information leakage, the hit count K has Binomial(n,1/4) distribution. The one-sided exact chance tail is

\[P(K\ge k)=\sum_{j=k}^{n}{n\choose j}(1/4)^j(3/4)^{n-j}.\]

This is a probability under a stated null, not a posterior probability that an extraordinary mechanism exists. Nonuniform targets, correlated draws, optional stopping, multiple scoring systems, or selective reporting change the null. Missing reports need a frozen rule; keeping them as misses is conservative for the fixed-n success test, while dropping them after reveal is invalid.

The sixth-round simulator should verify the exact distribution, compare fixed stopping with a clearly labeled selection demonstration, and include a deliberately leaked target as a positive control showing that above-chance scores do not identify the source of information. Primary inference must remain fixed-n and prespecified. A static public website cannot enforce target secrecy against its own reader: any in-browser hidden-target widget is a demonstration, not an independently blinded experiment.

## Selected sources actually read

**DREAM-2021.** Konkoly et al., *Real-time dialogue between experimenters and dreamers during REM sleep*, Current Biology (2021), DOI 10.1016/j.cub.2021.01.026. URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8162929/ . Read summary, introduction and results through the physiological-recording discussion. They report 29 correct responses across six of 36 participants under monitored REM procedures. Supports separating physiological verification and sensory communication from retrospective reports; no hidden-target or astral mechanism was tested. Full supplement was not reviewed.

**OBE-2007.** Ehrsson, *The Experimental Induction of Out-of-Body Experiences*, Science (2007), DOI 10.1126/science.1142175. Author-hosted manuscript and supporting information: https://www.neuro.ki.se/ehrsson/pdfs/Ehrsson-Science-2007-with-SOM.pdf . Read main page and supporting methods/controls through the discussion. The study varies multisensory perspective and synchrony, measuring reports and skin-conductance responses. Supports an operational body-location-illusion lane; does not test remote target acquisition or explain every spontaneous OBE.

**DREAM-2026.** Elce et al., *Individual traits and experiences predict the content of dreams*, Communications Psychology 4,69 (28 April 2026), DOI 10.1038/s44271-026-00447-2. URL: https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-026-00447-2 . Read abstract, limitations, data/code availability and bibliographic metadata. The authors distinguish linguistic/report regularities from underlying dream generation and retain correlational limitations. This motivates locking reports and keeping interpretation separate; their code/data was located but not executed here.

**DREAM-DATA-2025.** Wong et al., *A dream EEG and mentation database*, Nature Communications (2025), DOI 10.1038/s41467-025-61945-1. URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12350935/ . Search/abstract-level record inspected; detailed reopening was intermittently blocked. A shared EEG/MEG/report data resource is a follow-up lead, not an analyzed dataset in this round. Do not label the collection imported or validated until its files, access conditions and actual recordings have been inspected.

The physiological literature and mathematical null calculations answer different questions. None of these readings establishes an astral-travel mechanism.
